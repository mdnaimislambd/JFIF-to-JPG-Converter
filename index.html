What is a JFIF file?

JFIF (JPEG File Interchange Format) is the original container format for JPEG images. A .jfif file is already a JPEG inside — Windows and some browsers save JPEGs with the .jfif extension, which many apps and upload forms don't accept.

Converting to .jpg re-encodes the image with the standard extension that most websites, editors, and upload forms accept — including Office, Photoshop, and social platforms.

JFIF vs JPG — what's the difference?

  • Image data: Identical JPEG compression in both.
  • Extension: .jfif is legacy; .jpg is the standard today.
  • Compatibility: JPG is accepted by most editors and CMSs. JFIF often fails uploads.
  • This tool: Decodes the JFIF and exports a clean JPG. Re-encoding may cause slight loss — higher settings (92–100%) preserve more detail.

What is an M4A file?

M4A is an MPEG-4 audio file, usually encoded with AAC. It's the default format for iPhone Voice Memos, and it's also what iTunes and Apple Music downloads use. The quality is excellent for its size.

The catch: many car stereos, older media players, Windows apps, and upload forms still can't play or accept .m4a. Converting to .mp3 gives you the most widely supported audio format there is.

M4A vs MP3 — what's the difference?

  • Compatibility: MP3 plays virtually everywhere. M4A support is common but not universal.
  • Quality: AAC (M4A) is slightly more efficient than MP3 at the same bitrate.
  • This tool: Decodes the M4A and re-encodes it as MP3 in your browser. Re-encoding a lossy format loses a little quality — choose 256–320 kbps to keep it minimal.
